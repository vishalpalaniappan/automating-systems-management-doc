\subsection{Deterministic Intelligence and the role of Meaning}

Intelligence can be understood as a feedback loop encompassing the abilities to perceive, understand, retain, reason, predict, and act. Meaning plays a central role in this process because it establishes the ability to understand what is perceived and reason about what is retained to predict and act.

\subsection{Debugging Intelligence}

If this were to be seen as a brain, then the processes established so far in this document ensure that the construction of the brain itself has the necessary meaning and is internally consistent. Then within the closed semantic world established by the brain, another layer forms where the brain itself is able to reason about the reality it perceives. These are two different layers, one layers reasons to establish another layer that has the ability to reason and learn. 

If reality refutes the assumptions of the closed semantic world established in the first layer, the same process continues to ensure the world has correct meaning. However, if reality reveals that the meaning understood by the second layer is incorrect, it can learn on its own. This is because the constructed closed semantic world has the necessary mechanisms in it to make this possible.

It is very resource efficient because given a situation that it has seen before, it can leverage memory to avoid redundant computation. CLP can be leveraged here to optimally compress the memory and access it efficiently without decompression because the domain is entirely unambiguous. 

Perceiving reality to understand its meaning remains a task that would be effective for probabilistic intelligence, for example, what is the meaning of an image or of a sound. However, once that meaning and the certainty of the meaning is understood, a deterministic process can reason about it. This means that these two forms of intelligence are not mutually exclusive and they excel at different things. When working together, they create a much more effective intelligence.
